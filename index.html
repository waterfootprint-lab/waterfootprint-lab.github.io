About the Lab

The Water Security Lab in the Department of Civil, Construction and Environmental Engineering at The University of Alabama studies how people use freshwater and what that use means for the security of water supplies, food systems, and ecosystems. The lab is led by Dr. Mesfin Mekonnen, a Clarivate Highly Cited Researcher who has published more than 70 peer-reviewed papers in journals including Science, Nature, and PNAS.

Freshwater is under growing pressure. Roughly four billion people live under conditions of severe water scarcity for at least one month of the year, a pattern shaped by both climate and the intensity of local water use. Rising demand for food and energy, prolonged drought, declining groundwater, and emerging contaminants such as PFAS add to this pressure and make water security a local, national, and global challenge at the same time.

Our work traces water from where it is used to where its impacts are felt. We quantify the water footprints of crops, livestock, and traded goods, assess water availability, scarcity, drought, and low-flow risk, examine groundwater and surface-water withdrawals, and map PFAS pathways and water-quality risk. These studies combine hydrologic modeling, remote sensing, geospatial analysis, and machine learning across scales from individual farm parcels and river basins to the contiguous United States and the globe.

The lab's research is supported by the National Science Foundation, NOAA through the Cooperative Institute for Research to Operations in Hydrology (CIROH), and the Global Water Security Center.
